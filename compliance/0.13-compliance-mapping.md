## 3. GDPR — Full Texts, Summarized

These are summaries of the official wording, with the operative requirement for AfterCast AI. The official EU text is the source for filing.

### 3.1 Art 5 — Principles Relating to Processing

**5(1)(a) Lawfulness, fairness, transparency:** Personal data is processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent manner. AfterCast AI has a lawful basis for every flow and tells subjects what it does.

**5(1)(b) Purpose limitation:** Personal data is collected for specified, explicit, legitimate purposes. It is not further processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes. Training a model on email as `user_id` when email was collected for forecasting is incompatible with the forecasting purpose.

**5(1)(c) Data minimisation:** Personal data is adequate, relevant, limited to what is necessary. Collecting NIN + BVN + Passport at application stage before interview is beyond what is necessary. Storing a full `SELECT *` dump is beyond what is necessary.

**5(1)(d) Accuracy:** Personal data is accurate and kept up to date. No process to correct or update stale copies in Dropbox, WhatsApp, Sheet is a gap.

**5(1)(e) Storage limitation:** Personal data is kept no longer than necessary. Flow 1 kept without limit, with retention 365 days, is a gap. Flow 2 rejected kept without limit, with retention 180 days, is a gap.

**5(1)(f) Integrity and confidentiality:** Personal data is processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security, including protection against unauthorised or unlawful processing and accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical or organisational measures. Anyone-with-link, admin / admin, no encryption are gaps.

**5(2) Accountability:** The controller is responsible for, and is able to demonstrate, compliance with 5(1). No RoPA, no logs, no DPO evidence are gaps.

### 3.2 Art 6 — Lawfulness of Processing

Processing is lawful only if at least one basis applies: consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, legitimate interests. AfterCast AI uses:

- HR: Contract, plus explicit consent for Sensitive
- Marketing: Contract via DPA, not Legitimate Interest + Consent alone
- Product: Contract

### 3.3 Art 9 — Processing of Special Categories

Processing of special categories is prohibited unless an exception applies, including explicit consent. NIN, BVN, Passport, and health data in Flow 2 are Sensitive. Explicit consent is the basis.

### 3.4 Art 13 — Information to Be Provided Where Data Collected from Data Subject

The controller provides identity, contact, purposes, lawful basis, recipients, retention, rights, at collection. Flow 2 Form had no privacy notice. NDPA Sec 27 mirrors it.

### 3.5 Art 15 — Right of Access

The data subject has the right to obtain confirmation of processing and access to personal data, with a copy. In the Before Architecture, access requires search across 13 copies. In the After Architecture, access is via `POST /dsr` + `consent-ledger` + single copy.

### 3.6 Art 16 — Right to Rectification

The data subject has the right to obtain rectification of inaccurate personal data without undue delay. In the Before Architecture, rectification across 13 copies is not possible with proof. In the After Architecture, rectification is via `POST /dsr` + single copy + audit log.

### 3.7 Art 17 — Right to Erasure

The data subject has the right to obtain erasure without undue delay where data is no longer necessary, consent is withdrawn, or data is unlawfully processed. With 6 + 7 copies and no inventory, erasure is impossible. In the After Architecture, erasure is S3 Lifecycle + Aurora `DELETE WHERE user_id = sha256_hashed_id` + DSR API + deletion proof.

### 3.8 Art 18 — Right to Restriction of Processing

The data subject has the right to obtain restriction where accuracy is contested, processing is unlawful, or the controller no longer needs the data. In the After Architecture, restriction is via `POST /dsr` + `consent-ledger` + access control.

### 3.9 Art 20 — Right to Data Portability

The data subject has the right to receive personal data in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format. In the After Architecture, portability is via `POST /dsr` + S3 + Aurora export.

### 3.10 Art 21 — Right to Object

The data subject has the right to object to processing based on legitimate interests or direct marketing. In the After Architecture, objection is via `POST /dsr` + `consent-ledger` + `withdrawn_at`.

### 3.11 Art 25 — Data Protection by Design and by Default

The controller implements appropriate technical and organisational measures, such as pseudonymisation, designed to implement data-protection principles, at time of determination of means and at time of processing. By default, only data necessary for each purpose is processed. Training on raw email, no pseudonymisation, no Lake Formation view are gaps. In the After Architecture, hash email to `sha256_hashed_id`, Lake Formation anonymised view, train on hashed ID only.

### 3.12 Art 28 — Processor

Where processing is done on behalf of the controller, the controller uses only processors providing sufficient guarantees, governed by a contract, DPA, that sets out subject matter, duration, nature, purpose, types of data, obligations, and rights. No DPA with LuxeMart that forbids WeTransfer, no DPA with Dropbox, WhatsApp, Google are gaps. In the After Architecture, DPA + TIA are signed with LuxeMart, processor contracts are in place for AWS, no personal SaaS is a processor.

### 3.13 Art 30 — Records of Processing Activities

Each controller and processor maintains a record, RoPA, with purposes, categories, recipients, transfers, retention, security measures. No RoPA in the Before Architecture is a gap. In the After Architecture, RoPA is `0.3-ropa.xlsx`.

### 3.14 Art 32 — Security of Processing

The controller and processor implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to risk, including pseudonymisation and encryption, confidentiality, integrity, availability, resilience, ability to restore, and process for regularly testing. Includes: encryption in transit and at rest, access control, logging, backup. WeTransfer no encryption, Dropbox anyone-with-link, QNAP admin / admin, SMBv1, USB no encryption, no MFA, no logging are gaps. In the After Architecture, TLS 1.2, KMS CMK, SSO + MFA, VPC Endpoint, CloudTrail, Backup KMS + MFA Delete.

### 3.15 Art 33 — Notification of Personal Data Breach to Supervisory Authority

In case of breach, the controller notifies the supervisory authority without undue delay, not later than 72 hours after becoming aware, unless unlikely to result in risk. No breach playbook, no logging, no DPO process in the Before Architecture means 72 hours is not achievable. In the After Architecture, EventBridge to SNS to DPO, Step Function playbook, forensics account.

### 3.16 Art 34 — Communication of Personal Data Breach to Data Subject

Where breach is likely to result in high risk to rights and freedoms, the controller communicates the breach to the data subject without undue delay, in clear language, with nature, consequences, measures. In the After Architecture, communication is via DPO + generic ticket + SNS payload with the 72-hour template.

### 3.17 Art 35 — Data Protection Impact Assessment

Where processing is likely to result in high risk, the controller carries out DPIA before processing, especially systematic and extensive evaluation with automated processing, profiling, large scale Sensitive data. Flow 1 profiling + Flow 2 NIN / BVN are high risk. No DPIA in the Before Architecture is a gap. In the After Architecture, DPIA — HR + DPIA — Profiling, `0.4-dpia-hr.md` + `0.5-dpia-luxemart-profiling.md`.

### 3.18 Art 37 — Designation of Data Protection Officer

The controller designates a DPO where core activities consist of large scale systematic monitoring or large scale Sensitive data. AfterCast AI designates Kenneth Pratt, Legal and DPO, reporting to CEO. Contact is `compliance@aftercast.ai`.

### 3.19 Art 44 — General Principle for Transfers

Any transfer to a third country or international organisation takes place only where the conditions in Chapter V are complied with, including adequacy, appropriate safeguards, SCCs, and TIA. Transfers to US with no SCCs, no TIA, no DPA in the Before Architecture are gaps. In the After Architecture, data stays in `eu-west-1`, with SCCs + TIA + DPA for any transfer.

## 4. NDPA — Full Texts, Summarized

These are summaries of the official NDPA 2023 wording, with the operative requirement for AfterCast AI. The official NDPA text is the source for filing.

### 4.1 Sec 24 — Principles of Personal Data Processing

**Sec 24(a) Lawfulness:** Personal data is processed lawfully, fairly, transparently, with a lawful basis.

**Sec 24(b) Purpose limitation:** Personal data is collected for specified, explicit, legitimate purposes, not further processed incompatibly.

**Sec 24(c) Data minimisation:** Personal data is adequate, relevant, limited to what is necessary.

**Sec 24(d) Storage limitation:** Personal data is kept no longer than necessary. Flow 1 365 days, Flow 2 rejected 180 days, hired 6 years.

**Sec 24(e) Accuracy:** Personal data is accurate, kept up to date.

**Sec 24(f) Integrity and confidentiality:** Personal data is processed with appropriate security, including protection against unauthorised processing, loss, destruction, damage.

**Sec 24(g) Accountability:** The controller is responsible for, and is able to demonstrate, compliance.

**Sec 24(h) Duty of care:** The controller exercises duty of care in processing. USB in unlocked drawer, QNAP admin / admin are gaps.

### 4.2 Sec 25 — Lawful Basis and Sensitive Data

Processing is lawful where a basis applies: consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public interest, legitimate interests. Sensitive data requires explicit consent. NIN, BVN, Passport require explicit consent.

### 4.3 Sec 27 — Privacy Notice

The controller provides a privacy notice at collection, with identity, contact, purposes, lawful basis, recipients, retention, rights. Flow 2 Form had no privacy notice. In the After Architecture, Privacy Notice for Careers, `0.15-privacy-notice-careers.md`, is live before Flow 2 intake goes live.

### 4.4 Sec 29 — Record of Processing Activities

The controller maintains a RoPA with purposes, categories, recipients, transfers, retention, security measures. In the After Architecture, RoPA is `0.3-ropa.xlsx`.

### 4.5 Sec 32 — Data Protection Officer

The controller designates a DPO where core activities involve large scale Sensitive data or systematic monitoring. Kenneth Pratt is DPO, reporting to CEO.

### 4.6 Sec 34 — Right of Access

The data subject has the right to access personal data, with a copy, without undue delay. In the After Architecture, access is via `POST /dsr` + single copy.

### 4.7 Sec 36 — Right to Erasure

The data subject has the right to erasure where data is no longer necessary, consent is withdrawn, or data is unlawfully processed. In the After Architecture, erasure is S3 Lifecycle + Aurora DELETE + DSR API + deletion proof.

### 4.8 Sec 39 — Security Measures

The controller implements appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure security, including encryption, access control, logging, backup, resilience. Anyone-with-link, admin / admin, no encryption are gaps. In the After Architecture, KMS, TLS 1.2, SSO + MFA, VPC Endpoint, CloudTrail, Backup KMS + MFA Delete.

### 4.9 Sec 40 — Breach Notification

In case of breach, the controller notifies the Commission and data subjects without undue delay, not later than 72 hours after becoming aware, where breach is likely to result in risk. No playbook in the Before Architecture means 72 hours is not achievable. In the After Architecture, EventBridge to SNS to DPO, Step Function playbook, forensics.

### 4.10 Sec 41 — Cross-Border Transfers

Transfers to third countries take place only where adequate safeguards exist, including adequacy, SCCs, DPA, TIA, and Commission approval where required. Transfers to US with no safeguards are gaps. In the After Architecture, data stays in `eu-west-1`, with SCCs + TIA + DPA for any transfer.

### 4.11 Sec 44 — Data Protection Impact Assessment

Where processing is likely to result in high risk, the controller carries out DPIA before processing, especially profiling and large scale Sensitive data. Flow 1 profiling + Flow 2 NIN / BVN are high risk. In the After Architecture, DPIA — HR + DPIA — Profiling.

### 11.1 Numbering System — Rules

- Phase 0 docs use `0.x`, sequential from `0.0` to `0.18`, with no gaps. `0.0` is prerequisites. `0.1` is Shadow IT Inventory. `0.2` is Audit Report. `0.3` is RoPA. `0.4` is DPIA — HR. `0.5` is DPIA — Profiling. `0.6` is Business Case. `0.7` is Foundational Roadmap. `0.8` is Roadmap. `0.9` is Glossary. `0.10` is Appendix B. `0.11` is Classification. `0.12` is Retention. `0.13` is this file, Compliance Mapping. `0.14` is DPA + TIA. `0.15` is Privacy Notice. `0.16` is Legacy Sanitization Certificate. `0.17` is Appendix G. `0.18` is Appendix H.
- Phase guides use `phase-N`, for example `phase-1-organization-identity.md`. They are not `0.x`, because they belong to Phases 1 to 10, not Phase 0.
- Evidence uses `phase-N`, for example `evidence/sections-after/phase-1/`. It is not `0.x`.
- Terraform uses `phase-N`, for example `terraform/phases/phase-1/`. It is not `0.x`.
- File names keep bare `before` / `after` where they appear: `audit-before`, `sections-after`, `0.18-appendix-h-before-vs-after.md`.

## 12. Rules — 1 Fact, 1 Name

- **1 Fact, 1 Name:** Character, Location, Count, DataClass match exactly across Inventory, Diagram, Findings, RoPA, DPIA, Tables, Appendix B, Roadmap. Auditor Ctrl+F finds the same name everywhere.
- **One fact, one file:** Glossary lives once in `0.9-glossary.md`. Compliance mapping lives once in this file, `0.13-compliance-mapping.md`. Other docs link.
- **Separation:** `0.0-prerequisites.md` is the operational checklist. `0.7-foundational-roadmap.md` is The Foundation, the story and narrative. They are separate files.
- **Audit Report name:** Security and Privacy Audit Report, or Audit Report, with no number in the name.
- **Phase vs Wave vs Section:** Phase = 0 to 10 roadmap block, 11 Phases. Wave = 1 to 5 delivery increment, 5 Waves. Section = single build unit inside a Phase, 56 Sections in the Roadmap.
- **Naming:** `phase-1` format throughout. `terraform/phases/phase-1/`, `docs/phases/phase-1-...`, `evidence/sections-after/phase-1/`.
- **Numbering:** All doc filenames use the numbering system in Sec 11.1. Phase 0 docs use `0.x`. Phase guides, evidence, and Terraform use `phase-N`.
- **Email separator:** Em-dash style. `Personal Gmail: funmi.personal@gmail.com — Funmi Daniels (Sales, AE)` and `WorkMail: funmi.daniels@aftercast.ai — Funmi Daniels (Sales, AE)`.
- **Fictional Gmail disclaimer:** The disclaimer appears on: cover, note after title, full disclaimer in Sec 2.2, footnote in Sec 4 / Table 3, footer, README, Appendix B.
- **Terminology:** DataClass tags are Restricted-PII and Restricted-PII + Sensitive. SPII is not a DataClass tag. Node (7-Node model) is used consistently. Transit A-F is used. Lifecycle Phases are: Collection, Ingestion, Distribution, Access, Replication, Retention.
- **After Architecture / Before Architecture:** Doc sections, titles, and table columns use After Architecture / Before Architecture. File names, in-AWS custom configs, resources, tags, and service names keep bare `after` / `before`.
- **RetentionDays:** Only 90, 180, 365, 1825. Pseudonymisation is a technique.
- **Counts:** The count is 143 total = 11 hired + 132 rejected.
- **Logs vs PII:** Logs + deletion proof are kept 6 years in Glacier Deep Archive. PII is not kept 6 years. Flow 1 PII is deleted 365d. Flow 2 rejected is deleted 180d, hired is kept 6y.
