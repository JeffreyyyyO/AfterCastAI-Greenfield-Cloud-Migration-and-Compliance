## **1\. Executive Summary** {#1.-executive-summary}

### **Overview**

This report presents the formal security and privacy audit of AfterCast AI’s IT infrastructure, data handling practices, and compliance controls for **Flow 1: LuxeMart UK**, **12,000** EU customer records, and **Flow 2: Nigerian hiring**, **143** applicant records containing NIN, BVN, passport and bank details. Using a **7-Node** retrospective audit, we mapped every copy at rest and every transit gap. We found **13 uncontrolled copies** across personal Gmail, Dropbox, WhatsApp, Google Sheets, personal devices, QNAP NAS, USB, and a public GitHub repository, resulting in **14 findings: 7 Critical and 7 High.** The gaps breach GDPR Articles 5, 25, 28, 32 and 33, and NDPA 2023 Sections 24, 25, 32, 40, 41 and 44\. This report sets out a single-copy To-Be architecture in AWS eu-west-1 with S3 KMS encryption, Lake Formation, WorkMail, and lifecycle deletion, plus a phased remediation roadmap to close all Critical risks within **7 days.**

### **Total Uncontrolled Data Copies Found:**

The following redundant data copies were found in both data flows (which constitute this audit scope).

* **Flow 1:** LuxeMart UK Customer Data (12,000): 6 copies \- 5 locations still live \+ 1 copy leaked to public GitHub via trained model.  
* **Flow 2:** Nigerian Applicants Hiring Sensitive Data (NIN & BVN; 143 Applicants): 7 copies \- 4 locations still live in personal Gmail accounts, 1 copy in Dell Laptop, 1 copy in the QNAP  Network Attached Storage (NAS), 1 copy in USB backup kept inside unlocked drawer.

### **Critical Risks: 7**

1. **Personal Gmail, No Corporate Identity: A** Sales staff receives 12,000 European Union customer records via WeTransfer to a personal Gmail account. There is no corporate revocation and no audit log. If the Gmail account is compromised, the full LuxeMart UK customer file is exposed.  
2. **Dropbox Anyone-With-Link, United States:** The LuxeMart UK CSV file is stored in a personal Dropbox folder with the sharing link set to ‘anyone with the link can view,’ hosted in the United States, with no encryption and no access log. Anyone who forwards the link can download 12,000 records containing email, phone, and location. This breaches the LuxeMart Data Processing Agreement and violates cross-border transfer rules.  
3. **Model With Raw Emails Pushed to Public GitHub:** Machine learning model file trained on raw email addresses is pushed to a public GitHub repository. The model memorizes Personally Identifiable Information, so the email addresses cannot be deleted from the model. This is a public, notifiable breach.  
4. Google Form Collects Sensitive Data With No Lawful Basis: A Google Form owned on a personal Google account by an HR staff collects NIN, BVN, and passport number from 143 Nigerian applicants with no explicit consent checkbox for Sensitive data and no Data Protection Impact Assessment. This is a lawful basis for Sensitive processing failure.  
5. **Google Sheet Open to 30 Staff, United States:** The Google Sheet titled *"AfterCast Hiring 2025 \- Responses"* has open edit permission for 30 staff, hosted in the United States, with no encryption and no need-to-know control. All 30 staff can export 143 records containing National Identification Number, Bank Verification Number, passport, and bank details. This is a Purpose Limitation failure.  
6. **Personal Gmail Email With Subject "BVN List":** An HR staff member attaches the sheet to an email with subject "BVN List" from personal Gmail to a Finance staff member’s personal Gmail. Sensitive data is signaled in the subject line and contained in the attachment, stored in Sent, Inbox, and All Mail folders in the United States. The Bank Verification Numbers can be used for SIM swap, fraudulent loan applications, and bank fraud.  
7. **QNAP NAS admin/admin \+ USB Drive in Unlocked Drawer:** A QNAP TS-431K device at the Lagos (Nigeria) office uses default credentials (admin:admin) with SMB version 1 enabled and no encryption. A SanDisk USB drive titled "PAYROLL BACKUP" containing 143 National Identification Numbers and Bank Verification Numbers in plain text is kept in an unlocked drawer. This allows physical theft and ransomware with no backup proof and no audit log.

## **3\. Methodology & Framework** {#3.-methodology-&-framework}

### **3.1 Audit Methodology**

This is a **Zero-Cloud Retrospective Audit**, which means no CloudTrail, GuardDuty, or centralized logging has been integrated. The methodology is manual-first, evidence-based.

#### **Step 1: Stakeholder Discovery**

Interviews with 30 staff, focusing on characters who own data:

1. Ifeoma Nnaji (People & Culture, HR department),   
2. Bola Tinubu (Finance Manager, Finance department),  
3. Amara (ML Engineer, Data Science department),   
4. Funmi Daniels (Account Executive, Sales department).

Investigations about the storage and transfer of customer and applicant data revealed locations like personal Gmail folders, personal computers, unmonitored WhatsApp groups, personal Dropbox accounts and other non-compliant, unmonitored, non-corporate locations.

#### **Step 2: Shadow IT Inventory**

Manual inspection of the Lagos office (192.168.1.0/24), interviews and investigations revealed 15 unique points:

1. WeTransfer link   
2. Personal Gmail account (Funmi Daniels, Sales AE)  
3. Personal Dropbox folder links (open viewer permissions)  
4. WhatsApp group chat (Dropbox Links shared)  
5. Personal MacBook (Amara, Data Science)  
6. GitHub public repository (model.pkl)  
7. Google Form (Personal, Human Resources)  
8. Google Sheet (Personal, open edit permissions, accessible to all Staff)  
9. Personal Gmail ‘Sent’ folder (‘BVN List’ attachment)  
10. Personal Gmail ‘Inbox’ and ‘All Mail’ folders (Bola Tinubu, Finance)  
11. Dell personal laptop (Bola Tinubu, Finance)  
12. QNAP TS-431K NAS device  
13. USB drive backup (inside unlocked drawer)  
14. Gmail ‘All Mail’ and ‘Trash’ folders (infinite retention)  
15. QNAP NAS Trash folder, personal device WhatsApp backup & Google Drive backup of the Macbook (infinite retention of multiple copies)

The 15 inventory points were enumerated as 7 custody points and illustrated in subsequent data-flow map diagrams.

#### **Step 3: Data Flow Mapping**

We adopted the 7-Node model to separate Actor and Transmission nodes:

1. **SOURCE:** Origin Repo (Nigerian Applicants; LuxeMart UK).  
2. **ACTOR:** Custodian Node 1 (Ifeoma; Funmi \- personal Gmail accounts).  
3. **STORAGE 1:** Primary Repo (Google Sheet open to 30 staff; Dropbox open view permission links).  
4. **TRANSMISSION:** Distribution ("BVN List" subject email with sheet attachment containing PII stored in personal Gmail *Sent* & *Inbox* folders; open view permissions link saved in WhatsApp group).  
5. **ACTOR 2:** Custodian Node 2 (Bola; Amara \- personal Dell & MacBook laptops respectively).  
6. **STORAGE 2:** Secondary Repo (QNAP NAS, USB drive in unlocked drawer, local Postgres database, trained model .pkl file)  
7. **DISPOSAL:** Disposal Node (Trash folder infinitely; Gmail storage folders; QNAP Trash memory)

##### **Data Flow Key**

* Each Node (7) \= Data at Rest Node (or Custody Point).  
* Each Transit A-F (6) \= Data in Transit Gap.

##### **6 Lifecycle Phases**

1. Collection,   
2. Ingestion,   
3. Distribution,   
4. Access,   
5. Replication,   
6. Retention

#### **Step 4: Storage & Transit Gap Analysis**

* For each Node (Custodian node/Data at Rest node): Check for DataClass, Tag, Count Tag, Location Tag, Retention Tag, Encryption, Access Control, Logging.  
* For each Transit (Transition): Check for encryption (TLS 1.2, KMS), identity based access controls (Identity, MFA), Audit, and Data Loss Prevention.

### **3.3 Risk Scoring**

##### **Risk Matrix**

Risk Rating is calculated by combining Likelihood and Impact using the matrix below. The Risk Rating determines the remediation timeline.

| Likelihood / Impact | Low Impact | Medium Impact | High Impact | Critical Impact |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Low Likelihood** | Low Risk \- Monitor and review annually | Low Risk \- Monitor and review annually | Medium Risk \- Fix within 90 days | High Risk \- Fix within 30 days |
| **Medium Likelihood** | Low Risk \- Monitor and review annually | Medium Risk \- Fix within 90 days | High Risk \- Fix within 30 days | Critical Risk \- Immediate containment within 7 days |
| **High Likelihood** | Medium Risk \- Fix within 90 days | High Risk \- Fix within 30 days | High Risk \- Fix within 30 days | **Critical Risk \- Immediate containment within 7 days** |

* Low Likelihood × Critical Impact \= High Risk, fix within 30 days  
* Medium Likelihood × Critical Impact \= Critical Risk, contain within 7 days  
* High Likelihood × Critical Impact \= **Critical Risk, contain within 7 days**  
* High Likelihood × High Impact \= High Risk, fix within 30 days

**Likelihood:**

* Likelihood is **low** when: Corporate SSO with MFA is enforced; Device is managed by MDM with BitLocker disk encryption; Storage is encrypted with a CMK; & Access is logged to CloudTrail.  
* Likelihood is **medium** when: Passwords are enforced without MFA.  
*  Likelihood is **high** when: Dropbox links have open viewer permissions; Low security default “admin:admin” credentials are used on QNAP NAS; & Personal devices are used to process PII and Sensitive data.

**Impact:**

* Impact is **low** when: Public data is without PII.  
* Impact is **medium** when: Internal data contains limited identifiers .  
*  Impact is **high** when: Restricted PII (12,000) is kept beyond lawful retention .  
* Impact is **critical** when: BVN, NIN and passport numbers are collected. These can be used for a full identity takeover, bank fraud and  SIM swap attacks; Data is transferred to the US without adequate protection (violates NDPA Section 41); & A model file (model.pkl) is trained with raw emails and pushed to a public GitHub repository.

## **7\. Risk Assessment & Impact** {#7.-risk-assessment-&-impact}

### **7.1 Methodology**

**Likelihood** was rated **High** when data was accessible via:

* a public link that anyone with the link could open,  
* a Network Attached Storage device (QNAP NAS) using default (admin: admin) credentials with the obsolete file sharing protocol, SMB v1 (Server Message Block version 1\) enabled with no encryption, or  
* a personal Gmail account with no Multi-Factor Authentication enforced.

**Impact** was rated **Critical** when:

* the data involved was Sensitive data such as Nigerian NIN (National Identification Number), BVN (Bank Verification Number), passport number, and bank account details,  
* the data involved was 12,000 European Union customer records containing email addresses, phone numbers, and location data,  
* data was transferred outside Nigeria to the United States without adequate protection, or when there was no proof of deletion.

Risk Rating is **Likelihood** multiplied by **Impact.** Before remediation, there were seven **Critical** risks and seven **High** risks.

After implementing the compliant architecture, there are zero Critical risks.

### **7.3 Impact Analysis**

#### **1\. Confidentiality Impact \- Critical:**

**Flow 1:** With 6 copies of the CSV file, an attacker needs to compromise only the weakest link. The chain is Dropbox link with anyone-with-link access \> WhatsApp backup to Google Drive \> MacBook local Postgres database \> GitHub public repository. European Union email addresses plus phone numbers plus location data enable phishing attacks and trigger a notifiable breach under GDPR Article 33\.

**Flow 2:** With 7 copies of the Sheet, an attacker needs to compromise only the weakest link. The chain is Google Sheet open to 30 staff \> Gmail email with subject "BVN List" \> Finance Dell laptop \> QNAP NAS with username admin and password admin \> USB drive in unlocked drawer. National Identification Number plus Bank Verification Number plus passport number equals full identity takeover, which enables Nigerian banking fraud, SIM card swap attacks, and unauthorized loan applications.

#### **2\. Integrity Impact \- High:**

**Flow 1:** The MacBook local Postgres database uses a query that selects all columns including the entire email column, with no validation. Corrupted data is imported into the model file `model.pkl` with no checksum and no version truth. There is no Lake Formation control to prove which version is correct.

**Flow 2:** The QNAP NAS uses Server Message Block version 1 with no checksum, which allows silent corruption. There is no Lake Formation control, so 30 staff members can edit the Google Sheet and change Bank Verification Numbers with no version history showing who made the change and no ability to restore the correct data.

#### **3\. Availability Impact \- High:**

**Flow 1:** The MacBook is a single point of failure for the secondary copy. There is no Amazon Web Services Backup, no Multi-Factor Authentication Delete protection, and no versioning. If the MacBook is lost or stolen, the secondary copy is lost with no recovery proof.

**Flow 2:** The QNAP NAS is a single point of failure. There is no Amazon Web Services Backup, no Multi-Factor Authentication Delete protection, and no versioning. A power loss in the Yaba office at 192.168.1.0/24 makes the QNAP device unavailable and all Human Resources data becomes unavailable. The USB drive in the unlocked drawer is a single copy that can be lost with no recovery proof.

#### **4\. Regulatory Impact \- Critical:**

**GDPR failures:**

* Article 5(1)(f): Integrity and Confidentiality fail (Dropbox link allows unauthorized processing);  
* Article 32: Security of Processing fails (no encryption at rest with CMK key and no audit logging);  
* Article 33: Breach notification (72h window) fail (GitHub public repository was undetected);  
* Article 28: Processor fails (No DPA with Google/Dropbox/WhatsApp; LuxeMart UK can sue, ICO complaint can lead to a fine).

**NDPA (2023) failures:**

* Section 24: Principles fails (including purpose limitation and storage limitation);  
* Section 25: Lawful Basis fails (there is no explicit consent for Sensitive data);  
* Section 32: Designation of Data Protection Officer fails (No DPO oversight from Kenneth Pratt);  
* Section 35: Withdrawal of Consent fails (there is no easy way to withdraw);  
* Section 40: 72-hour Breach Notification window fails (there is no detection);  
* Section 41: Cross-Border Transfer fails (data is transferred to the US with no adequate protection and no Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs);  
* GAID 2025 Article 15: With NDPC classification as Data Controller of Major Importance (DCMI), violating Section 24 attracts penalties like fines up to 2% gross revenue, profit returns and mandatory audits.

#### **5\. Business & Reputational Impact:**

**Flow 1:** **LuxeMart UK** is a **European Union** client that requires data residency in **eu-west-1.** The Before architecture stores data in the United States, which directly breaches the contract clause and can cause **immediate termination of the contract**. The Before architecture demonstrates a lack of privacy by design.

**Flow 2:** If **143** Bank Verification Numbers leak, AfterCast AI cannot legally process Nigerian Sensitive Personally Identifiable Information again without passing a mandatory audit. This harms future business potential with Nigerian clients and demonstrates a lack of privacy by design for Sensitive data.

**Overall Impact:** Flow 1 has higher total exposure because of volume \[72,000 exposure points (6 exposures of the 12,000-customer dataset)\]. Flow 2 has higher per-record severity because of sensitivity \[identity takeover vs phishing\]. Both are Critical and require containment within 7 days.
